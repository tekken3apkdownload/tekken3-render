Tekken 3 Controls and Combos: A Practical Starter Guide

Most people lose their first Tekken 3 matches for the same reason: they press everything at once. The game only has four attack buttons, and once you understand what each one does, the chaos turns into a system. This page is the short version of that system for Android players.

The four buttons, explained properly

Left PunchFast jabs and the start of most combo strings. Your safest button.
Right PunchPower strikes and launchers — the hits that pop an opponent into the air.
Left KickLow and mid pokes. Essential for stopping opponents who rush in.
Right KickSweeps and roundhouse kicks. Slower, heavier, and great against crouching blockers.

Every limb is a button, which is why Tekken combos read like sentences: jab (left punch), launcher (right punch), then keep the opponent airborne with alternating limbs. Add movement — double-tap forward to dash, double-tap back to retreat, tap up or down to sidestep in 3D — and you have the whole foundation.

Your first week: a simple plan

  • Days 1–2, Practice mode: pick one fighter and learn three things only — a jab string, one launcher, and their throw (both punches together, close range).
  • Days 3–4, Arcade on easy: use only what you practised. Winning with four moves teaches spacing faster than losing with forty.
  • Days 5–7, Survival mode: limited health between fights forces you to block. Blocking — simply holding back — is the skill that separates Tekken players from button-mashers.
Touchscreen tip: in the Android version's control settings, enlarge the buttons slightly and place them where your thumbs rest naturally. Most missed inputs on a phone are layout problems, not skill problems. A Bluetooth controller removes the issue entirely if you have one.

Choosing who to learn first

Forgiving starters: Jin Kazama (balanced, teaches fundamentals), Paul Phoenix (huge damage on simple inputs), and Ling Xiaoyu (evasive, rewards movement). Eddy Gordo looks easy because capoeira strings flow from random presses — be careful, he teaches habits that stop working against real opponents. Get the game set up first

Everything above assumes the game is installed — the current Android build is about 61 MB, works offline, includes its emulator, and runs on Android 4.4 and above, so virtually any phone qualifies. Install it, spend ten minutes in Practice, and the next Arcade run will already feel different.
